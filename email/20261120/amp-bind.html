Tech and AI

Trump says U.S. firms won't be allowed to build data centers abroad

At a White House news conference, the president threatened a “tremendous tax” on companies that do. How it would work is not clear.

←
→
1 / 10

President Donald Trump said Friday that the U.S. will not allow American companies to build data centers outside the country, and that companies that try would face a “tremendous tax,” NBC News, The Hill and the BBC reported. He spoke at a White House news conference after returning from Asia.

“They allowed that to happen with chips and everything else, and it was very destructive to our country,” Trump said, according to NBC News. The Washington Post reported the announcement was a surprise; Trump said he had not told his staff beforehand.

The BBC reported that tech giants including Meta and Google have entered agreements to build data centers overseas, and that Trump named a build in Finland. Separately, the BBC reported Google announced a EUR 13bn AI investment in Finland in September. Google was contacted for comment, the BBC reported. NBC News and the BBC both report it is unclear how the policy would be put into effect.

Norma explains · analysis

The remark came at a press conference with no mechanism described and staff not told beforehand, so what it would do in practice is undefined.

The presidency

Trump names Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies national cyber director

She replaces Sean Cairncross, who CBS News reports was fired, amid a run of outages. She needs Senate confirmation.

←
→
1 / 10

President Trump has named Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon's chief information officer, as White House national cyber director, the White House announced. CBS News reported she needs Senate confirmation to serve in the role permanently.

She replaces Sean Cairncross, who is leaving. A White House official told CBS News he is leaving, and multiple sources told CBS News he was fired. CBS News, citing sources, reported chief of staff Stephen Miller had long wanted him out and selected Davies. Cairncross called the role "the honor of a lifetime."

The change comes during a run of outages that CBS News reported have hit Microsoft services, air traffic control, banks and the National Security Agency's internal network. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told lawmakers investigators have seen nothing indicating they are related, and said he is 98% confident they are not the result of malicious foreign activity.

Norma explains · analysis

The replacement comes as outages have disrupted several major systems, and the Washington Examiner reports the appointment as following them. But CBS News's sources say Miller had long wanted Cairncross out, and Mullin says investigators see nothing linking the outages. No source here says the outages caused the change.
